01 · THE PROBLEM

Why should every drone
do everything?

01

Disaster areas are dynamic

Victims, hazards, connectivity and flight conditions can change during a mission.

02

Single-drone dependency

A single fully equipped UAV creates a bottleneck for coverage, endurance and mission continuity.

03

Limited battery & connectivity

Tasks need to move to the UAV that is better positioned and still has enough endurance.

02 · PROPOSED SOLUTION

Multiple drones.
One shared mission view.

Instead of making one drone do everything, the system distributes responsibilities across Observer, Investigator and Relay capabilities while allowing task ownership to adapt.

O1

Observer

Wide-area monitoring and initial detection of people and hazards.

  • Area coverage
  • Victim detection
  • Hazard alerts
I1/I2

Investigators

Receive coordinates and move closer for verification and task execution.

  • Victim verification
  • Parallel investigation
  • Battery-aware return
R1

Relay / Comms

Supports the shared mission picture and communication flow in the prototype.

  • Mesh visualization
  • Status exchange
  • Low-bandwidth concept

05 · IMPACT

Designed for changing conditions.

01

Faster victim discovery

Multiple UAVs can search different parts of a disaster area instead of depending on a single drone.

02

Better battery use

Tasks can be assigned to the UAV that is better positioned and has sufficient remaining endurance.

03

Limited-connectivity operation

Important mission information can be exchanged through low-bandwidth communication concepts.

04

Mission continuity

If a UAV becomes unavailable, another available UAV can take over suitable unfinished tasks.
